Building AI Products People Actually Use

Novelty earns the first session. A clear job, reliable value, and graceful recovery earn the hundredth.

Many AI products attract attention and still fail to change behavior. The demonstration is memorable, but the daily value is uncertain. Adoption begins when a product solves a recurring problem more effectively than the workaround people already trust.

Find the painful moment

Do not begin with “Where can we add AI?” Observe where people wait, copy information, reconcile conflicting sources, repeat judgment, or abandon a task. A narrow, frequent problem creates a stronger adoption wedge than a broad assistant with unclear purpose.

Optimize time to verified value

Fast generation is not enough. Users need to reach an output they can confidently use. Measure the time from intent to verified completion, including review and correction. A slower answer that needs no repair may deliver more value than an instant draft.

Adoption survives the first bad answer when the product makes recovery easier than abandonment.

Design the recovery path

Show sources, preserve edits, allow undo, capture corrections, and provide a clear route to human judgment. One failure should not force users to repeat the entire task or guess how to phrase a better prompt.

Fit the existing environment

Meet users inside the systems and handoffs that already organize their work. A separate AI destination creates another inbox, another context switch, and another place to lose institutional knowledge.

Measure behavior, not enthusiasm

  • Do users return without prompting?
  • What percentage of outputs are accepted, edited, or discarded?
  • Which teams and use cases retain usage?
  • Does cycle time or outcome quality improve?
  • What happens after the system makes a mistake?

Successful adoption is quieter than launch excitement. It looks like a useful capability becoming an ordinary part of the work.
