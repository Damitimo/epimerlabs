Build vs. Buy for Enterprise AI

Buy commodities. Build differentiation. Design the boundary so today’s speed does not become tomorrow’s constraint.

The build-versus-buy question is rarely binary. Most enterprise AI products combine external models, vendor platforms, internal data, custom workflows, and purpose-built interfaces. The strategic decision is where ownership creates advantage.

Buy when the capability is standard

Vendor products are strong candidates when requirements are common, switching costs are manageable, integration is straightforward, and the capability does not distinguish the business. Buying can accelerate learning and reduce the burden of operating undifferentiated infrastructure.

Build where context is the product

Custom investment is justified when value depends on proprietary workflows, specialized data, unusual governance, deep system integration, or a distinctive user experience. Build the layer that encodes how your organization works—not a commodity model from scratch.

The best architecture preserves strategic control while borrowing commodity capability.

Evaluate the full cost

  • Implementation, migration, integration, and change management
  • Usage-based model and platform fees at realistic scale
  • Security, privacy, residency, and audit requirements
  • Customization limits and roadmap dependence
  • Monitoring, support, evaluation, and incident response
  • Exit cost: data portability, workflow coupling, and replacement effort

Design for optionality

Keep business rules, evaluation datasets, user feedback, and critical data models under your control. Use clear interfaces between models and applications. Test more than one provider for important tasks. Contract for data access and export before dependence grows.

Choose through evidence

Run a time-boxed comparison using representative tasks and production constraints. Score outcome quality, operating cost, implementation effort, risk, and user experience—not benchmark performance alone.

A good decision does not maximize ownership or outsourcing. It concentrates internal effort where it compounds and preserves the ability to change everything else.
