# Agency Research: Models for AlphaFox

A research brief on the top product/design agencies a new premium health tech studio should model itself after. Compiled for AlphaFox — a pharmacist-turned-PM founder building an AI-native, health tech specialist product studio.

---

## Top 3 to Model After

1. **[Topflight Apps](https://topflightapps.com)** — Your closest direct competitor. They already own "AI + healthcare" verbatim and prove the wedge wins. Steal their **named-integration vocabulary** (Epic, Cerner, Athena, HL7/FHIR) and **backed-startup name-drops** (Medable $91M, Tula Health $24.1M).
2. **[Metalab](https://metalab.com)** — Cleanest premium IA in the industry: logo grid → tag-filtered work archive → long-form case study with named team. Two-sentence hero. Real health tech receipts (Ro, Headspace, Hinge Health, CityMD) prove elite generalists win this market — so AlphaFox's positioning needs to clearly out-niche them.
3. **[Aleksandr Borisov](https://alexanderosso.com)** — Solo founder who already solved "AI-native and premium without looking like a no-coder." Names his stack (Figma + Cursor + Claude Code), shows slot scarcity ("1 of 2 open"), publishes fixed pricing ($4,500+ MVP, $3,500/mo retainer). Closest template for AlphaFox's first 12 months.

## Common Patterns Across Elite Agencies

- **Two-sentence hero, max.** Metalab, Locomotive, Work & Co, BASIC all do this. No paragraphs.
- **Outcomes as case-study headlines.** Ramotion ("Clearbit → $150M acquisition"), Eleken ("Aampe raised $18M"). The case study title is the result, not the client name.
- **Service taxonomy, not service menu.** 3–5 pillars (Brand / Product / Engineering) instead of a 14-item dropdown.
- **No pricing on site — but engagement models named.** Clay names "T&M, fixed, retainer" without numbers. This signals seriousness without scaring off enterprise.
- **Logo grid > testimonials.** Elite studios rarely show pull-quote testimonials. Logos do the work.
- **Founders are mostly invisible** at the elite tier — except for studios deliberately positioning around a founder (Borisov, Ueno's Haraldur era).

---

## Anti-Patterns (Mid-Market Tells to Avoid)

- "Trusted custom software development company" hero copy (Light-IT, Tateeda).
- "Project count" bragging — "3,000+ projects" reads as commodity (Bitcot).
- Pricing calculators / "Get a free estimate" (Tateeda, Cieden).
- 14-item service menu with everything from "video production" to "DevOps."
- Calling yourself a "vibe coding agency" externally (Octave, Makeitfuture).
- Platform partner badges (Make Platinum, Zapier Expert) — useful for mid-market lead-gen, fatal at the premium tier.

## Tactical Recommendations for AlphaFox

- **Copy Ramotion's outcome-headline case study format** because health tech has measurable outcomes (adoption %, time-to-diagnosis reduction, prescription-fill rate lifts, FDA submission acceleration) that translate even better than SaaS funding numbers.
- **Copy Topflight's named-integration language** ("We ship Epic, Cerner, and Athena integrations via HL7/FHIR") because integration vocabulary is the fastest clinical-credibility signal a website can carry.
- **Copy Borisov's transparent fixed pricing** ("MVP from $X, retainer $Y/mo, 1 of 2 slots open") because as a solo/small studio it converts faster than RFP discovery calls and signals confidence.
- **Copy Devvela's AI framing** ("We build with AI tools. We do not let the tool decide.") because it inoculates premium buyers against the "are you just GPT-wrapping?" objection without hiding the workflow.
- **Copy Metalab's IA**: logo grid → tag-filtered work archive → long-form case study with named team. Cleanest premium pattern in the industry.
- **Never use the literal phrase "vibe coding" on the site.** Internally it's the workflow; externally it's "AI-native build velocity" or "clinical AI product studio." The first reads as TikTok, the second reads as Andreessen.
- **Name the stack on an `/approach` or `/process` page, not the homepage.** Borisov names Figma/Cursor/Claude Code — but on a secondary surface. Homepage stays positioning-pure.
- **Lead positioning with the clinical wedge, not the AI wedge.** "Built by a pharmacist for health-tech founders" beats "AI-native health-tech studio" — because every studio now claims AI, but very few have a pharmacist-PM in the principal seat. The AI workflow is the *margin engine*, not the *headline*.

## On the USC Marshall MBA

Use the credential deliberately — don't lead with it.

**Where it helps:**
- **Pricing confidence.** MBA training in unit economics + willingness-to-pay analysis is exactly what most founder-led studios lack. Price for value, not hours — the single biggest premium-vs-mid-market divide.
- **Buyer fluency.** Health tech buyers at Series B+ are CEOs and CMOs who also have MBAs. You speak their language on cap tables, board dynamics, and GTM — agency conversations become strategy conversations, which command higher fees.
- **Credibility stack.** "Pharmacist + USC Marshall MBA + AI-native studio" is a three-part credential nobody else in this space has. Each piece compounds.

**Where to be careful:**
- **Don't put "MBA" in the hero.** It signals corporate, not craft. Elite studios sell taste and outcomes, not credentials. Keep it on the About page and LinkedIn.
- **Marshall over Wharton-flex.** Lean into the entrepreneurship angle ("Marshall-trained founder building from inside the clinic"), not the prestige play.
- **MBAs can over-strategize and under-ship.** The AI-native build stack is the antidote — make sure the site shows *shipped product*, not frameworks.

**One tactical move:** Tap USC's health tech and Lloyd Greif Center alumni networks, plus the Marshall Trojan founder community. Inbound from MBA classmates who become health-tech operators is the highest-converting top-of-funnel a founder-led agency can have. Worth more than any ad spend in year one.
